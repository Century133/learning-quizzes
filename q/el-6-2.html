6.2 Word order

Fixed word order

The sequence of words determines whether a sentence is grammatical or conveys a specific meaning.

Key terms

  1. Fixed word order - A linguistic property where changing the word sequence results in ungrammaticality or a change in meaning.
    • Languages including English, French, Chatino, and Choktaw.
    • Consequence of changing order is ungrammaticality or a fundamental shift in meaning.

Predicate function

The predicate describes the state, event, or activity attributed to the subject of a sentence.

Key terms

  1. Predicate - A word, usually a verb or adjective, that combines with a subject and potentially one or more objects.
    • Subject
    • Object

Transitivity patterns

Predicates are classified based on the number of objects they require to form a complete thought.

Key terms

  1. Transitivity - The number of arguments a predicate takes, also referred to as the valency of the predicate.
    • Intransitive: a predicate that takes one argument (the subject) and no object.
    • Transitive: a predicate that takes two arguments (a subject and a direct object).
    • Ditransitive: a predicate that takes three arguments (a subject, a direct object, and an indirect object).

Argument counts

Participants in a sentence are quantified based on the structural requirements of the predicate.

Key terms

  1. Arguments - Phrases that correspond to the participants or actors involved in a sentence's predicate.
    • Noun phrases
    • Prepositional phrases
    • Clauses

Object case

English employs specific morphological markings to distinguish pronouns acting as objects from those acting as subjects.

Key terms

  1. Accusative case - The morphological form used for pronouns when they function as direct or indirect objects.
    • me, us, you, him, her, it, them

Complex sentences

These sentences are characterized by a hierarchical structure where one clause is placed inside another.

Key terms

  1. Complex sentence - A sentence that contains at least one subordinate embedded clause.
    • Subordinate embedded clause

Clause identification

A clause serves as the fundamental structural unit that forms the basis of sentences.

Key terms

  1. Clause - A grammatical constituent consisting of a subject and a predicate.
    • Subject
    • Predicate

Flexible word order

Word sequence in certain languages is guided by stylistic choices or emphasis rather than strict grammatical rules.

Key terms

  1. Flexible word order - A property where word sequence is determined by topic, focus, or style rather than grammatical necessity.
    • Latin, Anishinaabemowin, Kanien’kéha, and ASL.

Names and studies

  • Dr. Kanatawakhon-Maracle notes that translating from English into Mohawk (Kanien’kéha) is not straightforward because flexible word order creates shades of meaning hard to distinguish in English.

Sentence functions

Sentences are grouped into categories based on the communicative intent of the speaker.

Key terms

  1. Sentence function - The classification of a clause based on its use as a statement, a question, or a command.
    • Declarative: used to make statements that can be true or false.
    • Interrogative: used to ask questions.
    • Imperative: used to express requests or commands.

Sentence structure

Sentences are classified according to the quantity and the relationship of the clauses they contain.

Key terms

  1. Sentence structure - The classification of a sentence based on the number and relationship of its constituent clauses.
    • Simple: a sentence containing only one clause.
    • Compound: a sentence containing at least two clauses linked by a conjunction.
    • Complex: a sentence containing a subordinate embedded clause.

Indirect objects

In ditransitive constructions, the third argument typically identifies the recipient of the action.

Key terms

  1. Indirect object - The argument in a ditransitive structure that represents the recipient of the direct object.
    • "to" (common marker)
    • Recipient (typical semantic role)

Interrogative types

Questions are formed using different structures depending on whether they seek a simple confirmation or specific information.

Key terms

  1. Interrogative clauses - Clauses used to ask questions, categorized by the type of answer they elicit.
    • Yes-No questions: e.g., "Did Romil watch a movie?"
    • Content questions: e.g., "What did Romil watch?"

Information structure

Word order in flexible languages is influenced by which information is being discussed and which information is being emphasized.

Key terms

  1. Information structure - The organization of a sentence based on topic and focus rather than fixed grammatical positions.
  2. Topic - The thing or entity that the sentence is about.
  3. Focus - The element in the sentence that the speaker wants to emphasize.

Compound sentences

These sentences consist of multiple independent clauses joined together by specific grammatical links.

Key terms

  1. Compound sentence - A sentence that links at least two independent clauses using a conjunction.
    • and
    • or
    • but

Basic word order

Most languages have a neutral sequence for subjects, objects, and verbs in simple declarative statements.

Key terms

  1. Basic word order - The standard order of Subject, Verb, and Object found in neutral declarative clauses.
    • SVO: English, ASL, both Mandarin and Cantonese, Nahuatl, and most of the Romance languages.
    • SOV: Japanese, Korean, Turkish, Farsi, Hindi-Urdu, Malayalam, Amharic, and Haida.
    • VSO: Irish, other Celtic languages, Anishinaabemowin, and some other Algonquian languages.

Names and studies

  • Matthew S. Dryer (2013) found that SOV (41%) and SVO (35.5%) are the most common word orders in the world's languages.

Recursive clauses

Language has the property of allowing structures to be nested within other structures of the same type indefinitely.

Key terms

  1. Recursion - The property of language that allows a constituent, such as a clause, to be embedded inside another constituent of the same type.
    • Seo-yeon knows [ that Danai laughed ]
