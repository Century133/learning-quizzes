8.4 Bones of the Lower Limb

Femoral blood supply

The proximal femur receives vital vascularization through a dedicated artery traveling within a supporting ligamentous structure.

Key terms

  1. Fovea capitis - A minor depression on the medial side of the femoral head that serves as a ligamentous attachment site.
  2. Ligament of the head of the femur - A weak intracapsular band spanning the femur and acetabulum that houses a nutrient vessel.

2.1 Artery to the head of the femur - The specific vascular structure carried by the ligament to supply the proximal end of the bone.

Muscle leverage

Bony projections of the femur and the sesamoid kneecap serve as mechanical levers to increase the torque generated by leg muscles.

Key terms

  1. Greater trochanter - A large lateral projection that provides a mechanical lever for muscles acting across the hip joint.
  2. Patella - A bone that increases the leverage of the quadriceps femoris by lifting the tendon away from the knee joint axis.

Femoral muscle attachment

The femur contains several specialized ridges and tuberosities that provide extensive surface area for the anchoring of hip and thigh muscles.

Key terms

  1. Greater trochanter - The large, upward projection above the base of the femoral neck where multiple hip muscles attach.
  2. Lesser trochanter - A small bony prominence on the medial aspect of the femur for the attachment of a single, powerful muscle.
  3. Gluteal tuberosity - A roughened area on the posterior proximal shaft extending inferiorly from the greater trochanter for muscle anchoring.
  4. Linea aspera - A prominent roughened ridge passing distally along the posterior side of the middle third of the femoral shaft.

4.1 Muscle attachments - The long, thin anchoring sites for multiple muscles of the hip and thigh regions along the ridge.

  1. Epicondyles - Medial and lateral irregular areas on the outer sides of the condyles that provide attachment for muscles and ligaments.

Patellar function

The patella is a specialized bone that protects the quadriceps tendon and optimizes the mechanical efficiency of the knee extensor mechanism.

Key terms

  1. Patella - The largest sesamoid bone in the body, found within the quadriceps tendon to prevent friction against the distal femur.
  2. Sesamoid bone - A bone incorporated into a muscle tendon where it crosses a joint to protect the tendon from wear and tear.
  3. Quadriceps femoris muscle - The large anterior thigh muscle whose tendon incorporates the patella and inserts onto the tibial tuberosity.

Patellar tracking

Proper patellar movement within the femoral groove is maintained by the structural morphology of the femur and balanced muscular pull.

Key terms

  1. Patellar surface - A wide groove on the anterior distal femur formed by the union of the medial and lateral condyles.
  2. Abnormal tracking - A clinical condition where the patella deviates laterally from its proper path on the femoral patellar surface.
  3. Lateral lip - A large expansion on the lateral side of the femoral patellar surface that prevents the patella from shifting laterally.

Pelvic width influence

The anatomical width of the pelvis dictates the femoral angle, which directly influences the lateral vector forces acting on the patella.

Key terms

  1. Q-angle - A measurement of the lateral deviation of the femur from the vertical line of the tibia, normally 10–15 degrees.
  2. Wider pelvis - A female skeletal characteristic that results in a larger Q-angle and increased lateral pull on the patella.

Weight bearing

The structural integrity of the lower limb is supported by two primary long bones that transmit the weight of the axial skeleton.

Key terms

  1. Femur - The longest and strongest bone of the body, which bears weight and constitutes one-quarter of a person's total height.
  2. Tibia - The medial bone of the leg and the second longest bone of the body, serving as the primary weight-bearer of the leg.

Tibial landmarks

The tibia features expanded condyles for knee articulation and several ridges and processes for ligamentous and tendinous attachments.

Key terms

  1. Medial condyle - The medial expansion of the proximal tibia that articulates with the medial condyle of the femur.
  2. Lateral condyle - The lateral expansion of the proximal tibia that articulates with the lateral condyle of the femur.
  3. Intercondylar eminence - An elevated, irregular area between the tibial condyles that serves as an attachment for knee ligaments.

3.1 Supporting ligaments - The two intracapsular knee ligaments that anchor to this elevated region of the proximal tibia.

  1. Tibial tuberosity - An anterior elevation on the proximal tibia that serves as the final insertion point for the patellar tendon.
  2. Anterior border - The subcutaneous anterior apex of the triangular tibial shaft extending inferiorly from the tibial tuberosity.
  3. Medial malleolus - A large medial expansion of the distal tibia that forms the palpable bony bump of the medial ankle.

Leg bone union

The tibia and fibula are held in close proximity by a fibrous syndesmosis and a strong sheet of connective tissue.

Key terms

  1. Interosseous border - A ridge running down the lateral tibial shaft and medial fibular shaft for connective tissue attachment.
  2. Interosseous membrane of the leg - A sheet of dense connective tissue that spans the distance between and unites the tibia and fibula.
  3. Distal tibiofibular joint - The articulation point between the distal end of the fibula and the fibular notch of the tibia.

Fibular function

The fibula functions as a non-weight-bearing lateral support that provides surface area for muscle attachment and stabilizes the ankle.

Key terms

  1. Fibula - The slender lateral bone of the leg that serves primarily for muscle attachments and does not transmit body weight.
  2. Lateral malleolus - The distal end of the fibula that forms the lateral bony bump of the ankle and articulates with the talus.

Ankle joint articulation

The ankle joint is a hinge articulation formed by the union of the talus bone with the distal configurations of the tibia and fibula.

Key terms

  1. Talus - The most superior tarsal bone, featuring a square-shaped upper surface that articulates with the leg bones.
  2. Medial malleolus - The distal tibial process that articulates with the superomedial surface of the talus bone.
  3. Lateral malleolus - The distal fibular process that articulates with the lateral side of the talus bone.
  4. Distal end of the tibia - The superior articulating surface of the ankle joint that meets the top of the talus.

Weight distribution

The body's mass is transferred in a linear sequence from the leg through the tarsal bones to the ground.

Key terms

  1. Tibia to talus - The initial transfer of body weight from the main weight-bearing leg bone to the superior tarsal bone.
  2. Talus to calcaneus - The subsequent transfer of weight from the talus to the heel bone which rests on the ground.

Tarsal support

The posterior foot is stabilized by specific tarsal bones that form the heel and provide a shelf for the ankle joint.

Key terms

  1. Calcaneus - The largest bone of the foot, situated inferiorly to the talus to form the heel of the foot.
  2. Sustentaculum tali - A prominent bony extension on the medial calcaneus that supports the medial aspect of the talus bone.
  3. Navicular - A tarsal bone that articulates with the anterior talus and the three cuneiform bones.
  4. Cuboid - A tarsal bone articulating with the anterior calcaneus that features a groove for a muscle tendon.

Foot curvature

The transverse arch of the foot is formed by the wedge-like geometry of the cuneiform bones and their distal articulations.

Key terms

  1. Cuneiform bones - A group of three wedge-shaped bones known as the medial, intermediate, and lateral cuneiforms.
  2. Wedge-shape - The structural configuration of cuneiforms with broad superior and narrow inferior surfaces to create curvature.

Metatarsal structure

The mid-foot is composed of five elongated bones that connect the tarsal region to the phalanges of the toes.

Key terms

  1. Metatarsal bones - Five elongated bones numbered 1–5 starting from the medial side of the mid-foot.
  2. Base - The proximal end of a metatarsal bone that articulates with the cuboid or cuneiform bones.
  3. Head - The expanded distal end of a metatarsal bone that forms the ball of the foot and articulates with a phalanx.
  4. Metatarsophalangeal joint - The articulation point between the head of a metatarsal and the proximal phalanx of a toe.

Phalanx count

The toes contain fourteen phalanges arranged in segments, with the first digit presenting a different anatomical count than the others.

Key terms

  1. Phalanges - The fourteen small bones found within the toes, numbered 1–5 starting from the medial side.
  2. Hallux - The first digit or big toe, which is shorter and thicker than the other digits of the foot.

2.1 Phalanx bones - The two specific bones, proximal and distal, that comprise the skeletal structure of the big toe.

  1. Proximal, middle, and distal phalanges - The three individual bones found in each of the toes numbered two through five.
