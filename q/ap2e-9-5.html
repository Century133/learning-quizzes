9.5 Types of Body Movements

Flexion and extension

These movements occur in the sagittal plane and involve anterior or posterior movements of the body or limbs.

Key terms

Flexion: A movement that decreases the angle between bones at a joint.

Bending a joint to move a limb anteriorly (or bringing the foot toward the posterior thigh).

Extension: A movement that increases the angle between bones and straightens a joint.

Straightening a joint from a flexed position or moving a limb or body part posteriorly.

Lateral flexion: The bending of the neck or body toward the right or left side.

Abduction and adduction

These involve medial-lateral motions of the limbs, fingers, toes, or thumb within the coronal plane.

Key terms

Abduction: Moving a limb or hand laterally away from the midline or spreading fingers and toes.

Raising the arm at the shoulder joint or spreading fingers apart.

Adduction: Moving a limb or hand toward or across the midline or bringing fingers and toes together.

Bringing the arm down to the side of the body or bringing fingers together.

Supination and pronation

These motions describe the orientation of the radius, ulna, and palm within the forearm.

Key terms

Supination: The forearm movement that turns the palm to the anterior-facing position.

The radius and ulna are parallel to each other.

Pronation: The forearm movement that turns the palm to the posterior-facing position.

The radius crosses over the ulna to form an X-shape.

Ankle movements

These movements are the only two motions allowed at the hinge joint of the ankle.

Key terms

Dorsiflexion: Lifting the front of the foot so the top of the foot moves toward the anterior leg.

Plantar flexion: Lifting the heel of the foot or pointing the toes downward.

Inversion and eversion

These movements occur at the intertarsal joints to stabilize the foot on uneven surfaces.

Key terms

Inversion: Turning the foot so the bottom of the foot angles toward the midline of the body.

Eversion: Turning the foot so the bottom of the foot angles away from the midline.

Scapular movements

These include anterior-posterior and upward-downward motions of the shoulder blade.

Key terms

Protraction: Moving the scapula forward, as when pushing against an object.

Retraction: Pulling the scapula posteriorly and medially toward the vertebral column.

Elevation: The upward movement of the scapula and shoulder.

Depression: The downward movement of the scapula and shoulder.

Complex movements

These involve movements where one end of a body region moves in a circle while the other remains stationary.

Key terms

Circumduction: Circular movement of a limb, hand, or finger using a combination of planes.

Sequential combination of flexion, adduction, extension, and abduction.

Scapular rotation

These rotations allow for full abduction or adduction of the upper limb and are defined by the motion of the glenoid cavity.

Key terms

Superior rotation: Upward motion of the glenoid cavity as the medial end of the scapular spine moves downward.

Inferior rotation: Downward motion of the glenoid cavity accompanied by the upward movement of the medial end of the scapular spine.

Rotational movements

Rotation involves a bone turning around its own axis or another bone at pivot or ball-and-socket joints.

Key terms

Rotation: The twisting movement of a bone or body region around a central axis.

Turning the head from side to side at the atlantoaxial joint.

Thumb movements

The thumb's unique structure allows for grasping and specific planar movements at the first carpometacarpal joint.

Key terms

Opposition: Moving the tip of the thumb into contact with the tip of a finger.

Reposition: Returning the thumb to the anatomical position next to the index finger.

Flexion: Bringing the thumb against the index finger or into the palm.

Extension: Moving the thumb away from the palm within the same plane as the palm.

Abduction: Anterior movement to a 90° perpendicular position pointing out from the palm.

Adduction: Moving the thumb back to the anatomical position next to the index finger.

Excessive movement

These movements go beyond normal physiological ranges and are often associated with trauma or injury.

Key terms

Hyperextension: The abnormal or excessive extension of a joint beyond its normal range.

Hyperflexion: The abnormal or excessive flexion of a joint beyond its normal range.

Whiplash injuries involving sudden backward and forward movement of the neck.

Mandible movements

The jaw moves to open, close, or protrude through vertical and anterior-posterior motions.

Key terms

Protraction: Pushing the lower jaw forward to stick out the chin.

Retraction: Pulling the lower jaw backward.

Elevation: Upward movement of the lower jaw to close the mouth.

Depression: Downward movement of the lower jaw to open the mouth.

Vertebral column

These movements result from the summation of small motions between adjacent vertebrae.

Key terms

Joint Types: Symphysis (intervertebral disc); Plane joint (between inferior and superior articular processes).

Motion Types: Flexion (anterior bending); Extension (posterior-going); Lateral flexion (side bending).

Limb rotation

These rotations are specific to multiaxial ball-and-socket joints at the shoulder and hip.

Key terms

Medial (internal) rotation: Turning the anterior surface of the limb toward the midline of the body.

Lateral (external) rotation: Turning the anterior surface of the limb away from the midline of the body.

Mandible excursion

These movements describe how the jaw moves relative to the midline.

Key terms

Lateral excursion: Moving the mandible away from the midline toward the right or left side.

Medial excursion: Returning the mandible to the resting position at the midline.

Joint structural limits

The range of motion is determined by the joint's structural type and supporting tissues.

Key terms

Ligaments: Fibrous tissues that constrain range of motion or prevent extension beyond anatomical limits.

Structural type: The physical classification (e.g., ball-and-socket vs. hinge) that determines available movement types.
