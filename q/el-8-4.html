8.4 Conversational implicatures

Logical Entailment

The relationship where the truth of one sentence guarantees the truth of another.

Key terms

  1. Entailment: A meaning that is necessarily true based on the preceding sentence.
    1. Sentence: "This is a book about the history of China" → Entailment: "This is a book."
  2. Non-cancellable: A meaning that cannot be denied without creating a logical contradiction.
    1. perp This is a book about the history of China, but it is not the case that this is a book. (Note: perp denotes logical contradiction).

Cancellable Meanings

The study of meanings that can be retracted or denied without creating a logical contradiction.

Key terms

  1. Implicature: A non-entailment that is not necessarily true even if the sentence itself is true.
    1. Sentence (2b): "This is a book about the history of China, but it is not the case that this book is long."
  2. Cancellable: The ability to negate a suggested meaning without resulting in a contradiction.
    1. Meaning "This book is long" can be retracted and clarified as "a short concise summary."

Contextual Influence

The way surrounding circumstances and speaker knowledge determine the meaning of an utterance.

Key terms

  1. Context-dependent: A property where a meaning changes based on the situation or participants.
    1. Context: Josh likes history → Implicature: "I think you will like this book"; Context: Josh dislikes history → Implicature: "I do not think you will like this book."
  2. Context-independent: A meaning that remains constant regardless of the environment or speaker.
    1. Entailment "This is a book" remains constant regardless of listener traits or environmental context.

Conversational Implicature

The specific type of implied meaning that stems from the application of dialogue-based norms.

Key terms

  1. Conversational rules: The underlying principles that allow implicatures to arise during interaction.

Names and studies

  1. H. P. Grice (1975) — Authored "Logic and conversation" to show that implicatures arise out of specific conversational rules.
