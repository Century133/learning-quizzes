9.4 Synovial Joints

Synovial membranes

As the most common type of joint, every synovial joint is functionally classified as a diarthrosis.

Key terms

  1. Synovial membrane — a thin tissue layer lining the inner surface of the articular capsule.
  2. Synovial fluid — a thick, slimy lubricating liquid secreted by the synovial membrane.
    1. nourishment — the process by which this fluid provides nutrients to the avascular articular cartilage.

Articular cartilage

This topic describes the protective hyaline layer covering the ends of articulating bones.

Key terms

  1. Articular cartilage — a thin layer of hyaline cartilage covering the articulating surface of bones.
    1. Teflon® coating — a comparison used to describe how the cartilage allows bones to move smoothly without damage.

Ligamentous support

This topic explores the fibrous connective tissues that anchor bones and limit abnormal movement.

Key terms

  1. Ligaments — strong bands of fibrous connective tissue that hold bones together.
  2. Extrinsic ligament — a supporting band located outside of the articular capsule.
  3. Intrinsic ligament — a supporting band fused to or incorporated into the wall of the articular capsule.
  4. Intracapsular ligament — a supporting band located inside the articular capsule.

Fibrocartilage structures

This topic covers the specialized pads found within specific joints for fit and shock absorption.

Key terms

  1. Articular disc — a small, oval-shaped fibrocartilage structure located between articulating bones.
    1. sternoclavicular joint — an example of a joint containing an articular disc.
  2. Meniscus — a larger, C-shaped fibrocartilage structure providing shock absorption.
    1. knee joint — the specific location where menisci provide cushioning between bones.

Bursa locations

This topic identifies the fluid-filled sacs that prevent friction between various body tissues.

Key terms

  1. Bursa — a thin connective tissue sac filled with lubricating liquid.
  2. Subcutaneous bursa — a sac located between the skin and an underlying bone.
    1. prepatellar bursa — an example located over the kneecap.
    2. olecranon bursa — an example located at the bony tip of the elbow.
  3. Submuscular bursa — a sac found between a muscle and bone or between adjacent muscles.
    1. trochanteric bursa — an example found at the lateral hip.
  4. Subtendinous bursa — a sac located between a muscle tendon and bone.
    1. subacromial bursa — an example protecting the shoulder muscle tendon.
    2. suprapatellar bursa — an example separating the anterior thigh muscle tendon from the distal femur.

Tendon protection

This topic addresses the structures that facilitate the smooth passage of tendons over joints.

Key terms

  1. Tendon — the dense connective tissue structure that attaches a muscle to bone.
  2. Tendon sheath — a small connective tissue sac containing lubricating fluid that surrounds a muscle tendon.

Pivot joints

This topic describes joints allowing rotation around a single axis.

Key terms

  1. Pivot joint — a uniaxial diarthrosis where a rounded bone rotates within a ring.
    1. atlantoaxial joint — the joint between C1 and C2 vertebrae allowing head rotation.
    2. proximal radioulnar joint — a joint allowing forearm rotation via the radius.

Hinge joints

This topic covers joints that function like a door hinge to allow bending and straightening.

Key terms

  1. Hinge joint — a uniaxial joint where the convex end of one bone meets the concave end of another.
    1. elbow joint — an example formed by the humerus and the ulna.

Condyloid joints

This topic explains biaxial joints involving a shallow depression and a rounded bone end.

Key terms

  1. Condyloid joint (ellipsoid joint) — a biaxial joint allowing two planes of movement through an elliptical articulation.
    1. radiocarpal joint — a wrist joint between the radius and the rounded scaphoid, lunate, and triquetrum carpal bones.

Saddle joints

This topic details joints where both articulating surfaces are shaped like a rider's saddle.

Key terms

  1. Saddle joint — a biaxial joint where articulating surfaces are concave in one direction and convex in the other.
    1. first carpometacarpal joint — the joint at the base of the thumb providing opposability.

Plane joints

This topic explores joints with flat surfaces that allow limited gliding movements.

Key terms

  1. Plane joint (gliding joint) — a multiaxial joint where flat or slightly curved surfaces slide against each other.
    1. intercarpal joints — examples found between the carpal bones of the wrist.

Ball-and-socket joints

This topic describes the joints with the greatest range of motion in the body.

Key terms

  1. Ball-and-socket joint — a multiaxial joint where a rounded bone head fits into a concave socket.
    1. glenohumeral joint — the shoulder joint formed by the humerus and scapula.
    2. hip joint — the joint formed by the femur head and the acetabulum.

Osteoarthritis pathology

This topic examines the "wear and tear" degradation of synovial joints.

Key terms

  1. Osteoarthritis — the most common form of arthritis associated with aging and articular cartilage erosion.
    1. bone thickening — a response to damaged cartilage that produces rough or bumpy articulating surfaces.

Rheumatoid arthritis

This topic details the autoimmune condition that destroys joint capsules and membranes.

Key terms

  1. Rheumatoid arthritis — an autoimmune disease attacking joint capsules and membranes, potentially causing joint deformation and disability.

Gouty arthritis

This topic describes the inflammatory condition caused by metabolic byproduct deposits.

Key terms

  1. Gout — a form of arthritis from uric acid crystal deposition caused by overproduction of uric acid or renal excretion failure.

Joint replacement

This topic covers the surgical procedure used to restore function to severely damaged joints.

Key terms

  1. Arthroplasty — a highly invasive surgical procedure where articular surfaces are replaced with prostheses.
    1. prosthesis — artificial components used to replace worn or damaged bone parts.
